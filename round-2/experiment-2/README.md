This experiment fits two mathematical models (Zipf's law and Zipf-Mandelbrot) to word frequencies in Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice to see how well these classic linguistic laws describe real text.

## Full Summary

This artifact implements Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) fitting of Zipf and Zipf-Mandelbrot models to global word frequencies from Pride and Prejudice (Project Gutenberg eBook #42671). The novel contains 123,350 tokens and 6,425 unique word types. The Zipf model yields s=0.992 [95% CI: 0.991, 0.995] with KS=0.093 and R²=0.739. The Zipf-Mandelbrot model yields s=1.267 [95% CI: 1.258, 1.269], b=8.165 [95% CI: 7.943, 8.410] with KS=0.011 and R²=0.966. The Zipf-Mandelbrot model is strongly preferred (ΔAIC=21,462). Residual analysis shows Zipf-Mandelbrot dramatically reduces systematic deviations in the head and body of the distribution. Bootstrap confidence intervals (n=100) were computed for all parameters. Four publication-quality figures were generated using the aii-data-fig-gen skill: log-log rank-frequency plot, residuals by region, goodness-of-fit comparison, and parameter confidence intervals. Additional analyses include frequency distribution statistics, per-rank residuals for top words, goodness-of-fit by frequency bin, and bootstrap parameter stability.
