## Full Summary

Rigorous MLE-based fitting of Zipf and Zipf-Mandelbrot models to Pride and Prejudice word frequencies following computational linguistics best practices (Clauset et al., 2009; Corral et al., 2015). Uses MLE instead of OLS, KS statistic for goodness-of-fit, AIC/BIC for model comparison, and residual analysis binned by frequency rank. Results: Zipf-Mandelbrot (s=1.266, b=8.138) strongly outperforms Zipf (s=0.992, b=0) across all metrics — ΔAIC=21,449, ΔBIC=21,440, KS improves 8.4× (0.093→0.011), R² improves from 0.741 to 0.967. Residual analysis shows ZM captures head of distribution well (residuals≈0) while standard Zipf systematically underestimates high-frequency words (head residual~15). Independent verification with shuffled-text baseline confirms model comparison is not vacuous.
